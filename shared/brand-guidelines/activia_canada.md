# Activia — Canada On-Site Content Profile

Market: Canada (bilingual EN/FR — Canadian English + Québec French). Source: Danone Canada SEO Content Rules doc + "Gut Health Network" regulatory/legal guidance (Activia Canada, 2025-2026).

For general Canada-wide legal/vocabulary/language conventions that apply to every brand, see `general_legal_canada.md` — this file covers only what's specific to Activia.

---

## Brand Rules

- Probiotics are not "essential" — never say they are key to overall wellbeing
- Activia is **not** qualified as a source of protein — never claim it
- When describing the bacteria inside the gut, do not use the term "live cultures"
- Never make direct comparisons with other Activia products, other probiotic yogurts, or wellness brands
- Never associate the brand with direct health benefits — only the benefits of the *product's* probiotics/prebiotics (1 billion active probiotics claim, or the specific 4-symptom claim — exact claim language and conditions of use must be confirmed per use)

## Vocabulary

- "live cultures" → not permitted for Activia (use approved probiotic language instead)
- Follow all cross-brand bans in `general_legal_canada.md` (healthy, wholesome, benefits, wellness, guilt-free, treat, natural, low sugar standalone, etc.)

## Gut-Brain Connection Messaging — Regulatory Guidance

This is a live, actively-monitored messaging territory. Follow precisely:

**Permitted (factual, sourced):**
- The gut and brain are physically connected by a nerve.
- Stress/anxiety/depression can manifest as gut discomfort, and gut discomfort can worsen stress/anxiety.
- Gut bacteria produce neurotransmitters that send signals to the brain and body; ~95% of serotonin is produced in the gut.
- Disrupting gut microbiota can increase the chance of developing digestive issues or disease, which can be associated with GI and emotional discomfort.
- A diverse, nutritious diet — including fibre, fermented foods, and probiotic foods — can promote a healthy microbiome.
- Encouraging the audience to consult a registered dietitian for individualized gut-health nutrition advice.

**Never claim:**
- That Activia "supports the gut-brain connection" (oversimplified, not accurate)
- That Activia supports or improves mental health
- That Activia supports gut production of serotonin
- Anything about "leaky gut" — controversial, no formal health recommendations support it
- That probiotics/prebiotics/Activia can "cure," "heal," "fix," or "detox" the gut or microbiome
- That good gut health can cure depression or other mental illness

**Required disclaimer:** Any reference to probiotics and/or "supports digestive health" must be accompanied, visibly, by: *"Activia contains more than 10⁹ CFU Bifidobacterium lactis per serving, a probiotic that contributes to healthy gut flora."*

**Approved phrasing examples:** "helps to support gut health," "feeling good from the inside out."

**Who can say what:**
- Only registered dietitians or other healthcare professionals may provide scientific information or opine on controversial health topics (e.g. the gut-brain axis) — and even then, must stay factual, relevant, and educational.
- Non-HCP content (including general wellness content) may share non-controversial general wellness tips and should frame personal-experience language as personal ("for me," "I believe," "in my opinion," "helped me feel less bloated") rather than as a product guarantee.

## Product Facts (for accurate reference, not standalone claims)

- Activia is a probiotic yogurt, not a standard/conventional yogurt — studied since the 1980s.
- Exclusive probiotic strain: *Bifidobacterium animalis lactis* CNCM I-2494, present at >10⁹ CFU per serving.
- Other probiotic yogurts in Canada may contain *Lactobacillus casei*, *Bifidobacterium lactis*, and *Lactobacillus acidophilus*, but do not declare the specific strain(s) on-pack, so whether they were studied in humans for a specific benefit cannot be confirmed — this is a factual differentiation point, not a denigration of competitors (still never name them).
- Source for category context: Danone Canada Gut Health Survey (2024) found 56% of Canadians experience symptoms of poor gut health every month.
