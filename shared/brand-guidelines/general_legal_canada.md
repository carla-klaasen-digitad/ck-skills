# General Legal & Claims Guidelines — On-Site Content (Canada Market)

## Purpose
Canada-market equivalent of `general_legal.md`. Consolidated from the existing Danone Canada content rules (previously scoped to off-site guest-post work only — the legal/claims/language-convention portions below are not guest-post-specific and apply to on-site brand content too). Use `shared/brand-guidelines/off-site/general_danonecanada.md` instead for off-site/guest-post structural and tone rules (BL Type, no-CTA, article format, etc.) — this file covers claims law and language conventions only.

When in doubt about any claim — flag it for legal review before publishing rather than qualify it yourself.

---

## 1. Legal & Claims

- Have the target keyword (and secondary keywords) validated by legal before writing; request clarification in advance on which uses are permitted.
- Do not commit to a health benefit that has not been officially confirmed by the brand (e.g., low-calorie, healthy, vegan).
- Use precise, indisputable sources only (government bodies, recognized organizations). Sources must be recent — less than 2–3 years old.
- Do not disparage dairy or lactose-free products — the Danone portfolio includes both. Stay neutral, or position content as flexitarian.
- Never use third-party brand names or trademarked expressions (e.g., Oreo, Skor).
- Never use the trademarked expression "wholesome goodness" or similar.
- Satiety claims: never make them — there is no proof any product suppresses appetite.
- Never reference guilt around food, in any form.

## 2. Vocabulary — Never Use

| Term | Use instead |
|---|---|
| healthy | "nutritious" or "balanced diet" |
| fresh fruits | "tasty fruits" or "delicious fruits" |
| wholesome | "balanced diet" — generally refused outright |
| benefits (re: a product) | avoid entirely |
| wellness (re: a product) | avoid entirely |
| low sugar (standalone) | exact sugar content: "with only X grams of sugar per portion" |
| guilt-free | never use |
| treat | avoid — implies occasional-only consumption |
| satisfying | "nutritive" |
| crafted | "prepared" or "made" (implies artisanal method) |
| irresistibly | avoid |
| Made in Canada | "Prepared in Canada" only |
| Lactoseless | "Lactose free" |
| artificial additives | "no artificial colours and flavours" |
| "the best" / "top quality products" (superlatives) | "a good yogurt" / "quality products" |
| high-protein | "High protein" — always no hyphen |

- Do not say "fruit yogurt" if the product contains no real fruit (flavours only).
- Do not say "real ingredients" if the product uses only flavours.

## 3. Canadian English Conventions

- Spelling: colour, behaviour, favour, neighbour, labour, flavour, savour, centre, fibre, litre, metre
- Licence (noun) / license (verb); practice (noun) / practise (verb)
- Double the "l" before a suffix: travelled, labelled, modelling
- Keep -ize / -ization endings: organize, recognize, organization, customize
- Use "grey" not "gray"
- Never "flavor" / "savor" — always "flavour" / "savour"
- Ingredient lists: prefer abbreviations "tsp." and "tbsp."

## 4. Québec French Conventions

- Spell it « yogourt » (Québec usage), not « yaourt »
- Replace « snack » with « collation »; « pack » with « paquet »
- Small fruits always plural: « un yogourt aux fraises et aux framboises » (not « à la fraise et à la framboise »)
- Use "MD" in superscript instead of « ® »; use "MC" instead of "™"
- Use the decimal comma, not the point: « 5 g », « 1,42 L »
- Insert a non-breaking space before « ; : ! ? » and inside « guillemets »
- « riche en » → « à teneur élevée en »
- « rassasié » → « nourrissant »
- « Made in Canada » → « Préparé au Canada »
- Do not use « moins sucré » (comparison required) → prefer « faible teneur en sucre » or give exact grams
- In recipes, avoid « riche en protéine » → prefer « recette avec des protéines »

## 5. Units & Symbols

- Write "kg" — never "KG" or "Kg"
- Lowercase unit symbols: g, kg, mg, L, mL
- Footnote markers and trademark indicators must be superscript
- French: non-breaking space between number and unit — « 5 g », « 1,42 L »
- EN: ® and ™ | FR: MD and MC (superscript)

## 6. Cross-Brand Feedback (Client Confirmed)

- Satiety claims: never — no proof any product suppresses appetite
- Guilt around food: never reference it in any form
- High protein — always no hyphen ("high-protein" is incorrect)
- "Prepared in Canada" only — never "Made in Canada"
